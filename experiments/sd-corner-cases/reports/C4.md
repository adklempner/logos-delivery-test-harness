# C4: departures, ghost ads and dead registrars: report

**Verdict.**
- **A departed node stays findable for 19–23 min.** Its ads disappear at the
  registrar's next prune tick, which runs every E (900 s): **longer than one
  advert lifetime, never more than two** (P6 confirmed). The documented "up to
  900 s" is wrong.
- **Losing 24 of 85 nodes at once barely hurt discovery:**
  - lookups failed more for about 5 min (21 % vs 13 %, at the module's 10 s budget);
  - the remaining providers stayed findable at 99–100 %;
  - lookups then got *faster* because the fleet was smaller (P7 mostly refuted).
- One node never started (libp2p switch not up within 180 s), the same failure as C3's m55.

Data in `out/collect/sd-c4`, T0 06:11:55 UTC, 3000 s. Pins as in C1. The
bootstrap has `P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`; lookers make one call at a time. Image is
stock (the instrumented registrar is used only in C5).

## 1. Topology and schedule

| Group | Nodes | Advertises | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| boot | boot | – | – |
| stay | m1–m50 | `sd/s-{i}` | – |
| leave-12 | m51–m65 | `sd/g-{i}` | +720 s (`docker compose stop`: graceful) |
| drop-25 | m66–m89 | `sd/d-{i}` | +1500 s (graceful, 24 at once = 28 % of the 85 left) |
| lookers | m90–m99 | 4 of the 89 ids every 15 s, rotating | – |

- m34 (stay): `libp2p start` timed out and the switch was never started within
  180 s (`discoStart: switch not started`). Entrypoint `start FAILED`, exit 1.
  The boot storm of 100 nodes at 884 % fleet CPU.
- No daemon crash; no isolated node.

## 2. Ghost ads (P6)

| Group | Stopped | Last returned by a lookup (abs. min / median / max) | Ghost lifetime after the stop (median / p90 / max) |
|---|---|---|---|
| leave-12 | 12:00 | 21:17 / **31:18** / 31:43 | **19:11** / 19:41 / 19:43 |
| drop-25 | 25:00 | 24:59 / **46:01** / 48:08 | **21:01** / 22:31 / 23:08 |

- The ghosts end in tight clusters, at 31–32 min and 46–48 min. Those are the
  registrars' prune ticks: `maintainRegistrar` runs `pruneExpiredAds` every
  E (`service_discovery.nim:74-77`), counted from each registrar's own start at
  T0+0.5–2 min.
- An ad accepted at time a is removed at the first tick after a + E.
  - Its lifetime after the provider leaves is therefore between E − (time since
    acceptance) and 2E.
  - Here that came to 19–23 min.
- Nothing tells the registrars that a provider stopped:
  - neither a graceful stop nor `stopAdvertising` deregisters
    (`advertiser.nim:440`);
  - lookups keep returning the departed peer's record, with its addresses, until
    the prune.

**Consequence for clients:** a discovered peer may be up to about 2E stale.
Dialling ghost records (connection refused, or a timeout on a vanished IP) is
the normal case for a while after any departure. The harness README's "up to
900 s" understates it.

## 3. Dead registrars (P7)

Lookups for the `stay` services (the providers that stayed up) by phase:

| Phase | Fleet | Lookups | Fail % | p50 / p95 ms | Probe median ms | Fleet CPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–12 min | 100 | 563 | 18 % | 4525 / 9441 | 645 | 884 % |
| 12–25 | 85 | 796 | 13 % | 3087 / 8643 | 384 | 838 % |
| **25–30 (24 dropped)** | 61 | 385 | **21 %** | 3954 / 9839 | 178 | 654 % |
| 30–40 | 61 | 716 | 8 % | 1112 / 6493 | 170 | 691 % |
| 40–50 | 61 | 868 | **0 %** | 979 / 1954 | 158 | 606 % |

- **The drop costs one bad window.**
  - For about 5 min, lookups fail more (21 %): a bucket whose picked registrars
    are dead waits its full 5 s deadline, pushing the lookup over the 10 s module
    budget.
  - It is fully over by 40 min, about 15 min after the drop. The dead peers'
    dial failures evict them, and the advertiser maintenance replaces lost
    registrations.
- **Findability of the stayers did not drop:** hit rate 98–100 % from 20 min to the end.
  With up to 48 registrars per service and about 30 % lost, every service kept
  enough live ads.
- **A smaller fleet is a faster fleet on this host:** after the departures,
  failures went to 0 and p95 fell from 9.4 s to 2.0 s.
- The 0–12 min window is worse than the drop itself (18 % failures). Boot-time
  load at 100 nodes costs more than losing 28 % of the registrars.

## 5. Daemon crash check

C3 suggested that discovery calls ending in a timeout precede the daemon
segfault. Here lookers had up to 103 module-side lookup timeouts per 5 min and
**none crashed**. So timeouts alone are not sufficient.

The crashes so far (C1 first attempt, C1b, C3) all happened at 87–99 nodes
under heavy CPU saturation (> 800 %). This run's worst window had the same
saturation and no crash.
