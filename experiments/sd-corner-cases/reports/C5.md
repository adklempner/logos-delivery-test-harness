# C5: why registrations are rejected (C1b replay, instrumented registrar): report

**Verdict.**
- **Every registration rejection is a ticket retry arriving after its 1 s
  window.** P5 is now proven, not inferred.
- The retries were **1–17 s late** (median 3 s, p90 9 s), and **51 % of all
  ticket retries were rejected**.
- The registrar's own numbers also confirm P1: an advertiser whose IP the
  registrar already holds gets a median **957 s** ticket, against 319 s for
  everyone else.

Data in `out/collect/sd-c5`, T0 07:03:09 UTC, 2100 s, `COLLECT_LOGS=all`.
Registrar lines were harvested every 120 s into `reglog/` (docker rotates logs
at 5 × 20 MB).

## 1. What changed vs C1b

- **libp2p_module:** `33e3c7a7` rebuilt against nim-libp2p `1b893898` plus one
  local patch, on branch `experimental/sd-registrar-reasons` (scratch worktree,
  uncommitted; source copy in `out/nim-libp2p-reglog`). The patch only adds

    cachedAds serviceAds ipScore`.
  - No behaviour change.
- **Image:** `logos-sim:reglog`. `logos-sim:local` was re-tagged to the stock
  image afterwards. **`out/modules/libp2p_module` still holds the instrumented
  build:** the next `./harness.sh build` restores the stock one.
- **Topology:** C1b's (87 nodes, 2 hubs with 43 services, 40 solo, 20 ctrl,
  4 late at +20 min, 10 delivery bystanders), with the 10 lookers all
  sequential. Bootstrap `P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`. Run 35 min.

## 2. Rejection reasons (all registrars, 35 min)

| Reason | Count |
|---|---|
| `invalid ticket: ticket outside valid time window` | **1632 (100 %)** |
| invalid advertisement / no register message / other ticket errors | 0 |

How late the retry arrived (`now − windowEnd`, seconds, timed in whole seconds):

| Late by | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4–5 | 6–8 | 9–12 | 13–17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share | ~30 % | ~10 % | ~8 % | ~8 % | ~20 % | ~15 % | ~6 % |

58 % were at most 3 s late; none arrived early.

- **The window is `[tMod + tWait, tMod + tWait + 1 s]`** (`registrar.nim:205-217`).
  The advertiser sleeps exactly `tWait` after it *receives* the ticket
  (`advertiser.nim:322-333`), then sends the retry.
  - The ticket's issue time `tMod` is truncated to the second.
  - Round-trip time and stream setup are not budgeted at all.
  - On a loaded node the event loop is late by seconds: probe latency had a
    median of 0.4–0.8 s and spikes to 15 s in C3/C4.
  - So the retry routinely arrives more than one second after `tMod + tWait`.
- **A rejection is expensive:**
  - the advertiser drops the task (`Rejected → return`, `advertiser.nim:334`);
  - the registrar slot stays empty until the next 10-min `maintainRegistrations`;
  - the next attempt starts **without** the ticket, so all accumulated wait is lost.
  - This is why rejections come in waves 10 min apart in every run.

|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (the registrar already caches an ad from this IP) | 555 | **957 s** |
| < 1.0 (median score 0.31 = 10 of 32 prefix bits) | 4395 | 319 s |

- **P1, measured:**
  - The 555 attempts at ipScore 1.0 are a node's second or later service at a
    registrar that already holds one of its ads (the hubs and their 42 extra
    services), or a renewal that finds its own unpruned ad.
  - Those attempts wait about E × occupancy (≈ 957 s): a full advert lifetime
    before the ad even exists.
- An ordinary advertiser in the `/8` scatter scores 0.31 and waits about 5 min. That is the floor
  of time-to-findable in every case.

## 5. Daemon crash, again: the clearest trace so far

Hub m2 died with `Segmentation fault logoscore daemon` (exit 139) at about T0+4 min:
1. Its `libp2p start` took **89 s** under the boot storm
   (`ffi request still in flight; caller notified via RET_STALE_WARN … Libp2pStartReq elapsedMs=80000`).
2. The driver then issued its 43 `discoStartAdvertising` calls at once.
3. All 43 failed, the slowest after **22.7 s**, past logoscore's ~20 s RPC deadline.
4. The daemon segfaulted seconds later.

The same sequence was seen in C1b's 4 parallel lookers: concurrent calls into a
busy libp2p_module, calls overrunning the RPC deadline, then a daemon segfault.

C3's sequential lookers died after back-to-back 10–13 s module-side timeouts.
C4's lookers survived the same kind of timeouts.

**Working hypothesis:** the logoscore daemon mishandles a module reply (or
event) for a call whose RPC has already timed out. Concurrency makes overruns
likely; a congested module makes them possible even one call at a time. A core
dump is needed to locate it.
