# C2: unreliable (flapping) advertisers among many common advertisers: report

**Verdict.**
- **Flapping makes a flapper invisible.** Once its first ads aged out, a node
  that re-advertises faster than the registrar's ticket wait was never
  registered again. The flapper with the longest on-period (120 s) was never
  visible at all.
- **Flappers also delay honest new advertisers of the same service.** Late
  joiners of the flapped service took 5–13 min to become findable, against
  0:09–5:22 for four of six joiners of the identical control service. The
  effect is moderate: 3 + 3 nodes per comparison.
- The fleet itself stayed healthy:
  - no isolation, no crash;
  - all 70 initial providers found within 5:40;
  - no resource leak on the flappers.

Data in `out/collect/sd-c2`, T0 04:24:31 UTC, 2700 s. Pins as in C1
(module `586e1a38`, libp2p_module `33e3c7a7` / nim-libp2p `1b893898`,
delivery `6b1f3a09`). The bootstrap has `P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`; lookers make one
call at a time.

## 1. Topology

100 nodes. 98 reported to Prometheus, all with non-empty service tables.

| Group | Nodes | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| boot | boot | sd-agent bootstrap |
| common-a | m1–m35 | advertise `sd/common-a` (the flapped service) |
| common-b | m36–m70 | advertise `sd/common-b` (control) |
| flap-fast / flap-mid / flap-slow | m71 / m72 / m73 | `sd/common-a`, 5 s on / 5 s off, 30/10, 120/30 |
| late-a-12, late-b-12 | m74–76, m77–79 | start +720 s |
| late-a-25, late-b-25 | m80–82, m83–85 | start +1500 s |
| lookers | m86–m93 | `sd/common-a`, `sd/common-b` every 15 s |
| delivery | m94–m99 | bystanders |

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Initial providers

| Group | Found | Median | p90 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| common-a | 35/35 | 4:01 | 5:05 | 5:40 |
| common-b | 35/35 | 4:04 | 5:00 | 5:10 |

### 2.2 Late joiners: time-to-findable, from first successful advertise to first lookup returning it

| Join | `common-a` (flapped) | `common-b` (control) |
|---|---|---|
| +12 min | 5:04, 5:38, 6:28 | **0:09**, 3:51, 15:22 |
| +25 min | 9:53, 11:43, 12:40 | **0:51, 1:51, 5:22** |
| all six | median ≈ 8:10, min 5:04 | median ≈ 2:50, min 0:09 |

- No `common-a` joiner became findable in under 5 min; four of six `common-b` joiners did.
- The gap is wider at +25 min, after 25 min of flapping, than at +12 min.
- This fits P3:
  - each flapper re-registration sets the service lower bound
    (`registrar.nim:139-160`) at the registrars it touches;
  - a new `common-a` advertiser must then wait out the remaining bound there.
- n = 3 per cell: treat it as a strong indication, not a measurement.

### 2.3 The flappers themselves

| Node | Cycles | Visible (returned / lookups) | First seen | Last seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flap-fast m71 (5 s / 5 s) | 255 | 327 / 1334 | 2:34 | **17:06** |
| flap-mid m72 (30 s / 10 s) | 67 | 403 / 1334 | 2:33 | **16:53** |
| flap-slow m73 (120 s / 30 s) | 18 | **0 / 1334** | – | – |

**fast and mid.**
- They were visible only from the registrations made in the first minutes,
  while registrar caches were empty: with ipSim 0 the wait is 0, so the ad is
  accepted at once.
- After about 17 min they never came back. Every later attempt meets either
  their own still-cached IP (ipSim = 1, a wait of about E) or a normal
  multi-minute ticket.
- A stop cancels the pending ticket (`advertiser.nim:440`), so it never matures.

**slow.**
- It started a few seconds later and never got an immediate accept.
- Its 120 s on-period is shorter than any ticket it was given, so all 18 cycles
  ended in a cancelled ticket.

**Disappearance at about 17 min: expiry or crowding?** At the same point,
lookups for `common-a` reached the `F_lookup` cap of 30 records (mean per lookup
29.3 → 30.0). The flappers' few early ads may have been crowded out rather than
pruned. C4 measures ghost lifetime directly.

**Resources: no leak.**
- libp2p_module CPU over 45 min: flappers 75–112 s, an honest provider (m5) 100 s.
- RSS: 38–46 MiB, the same as honest nodes.
- Advertiser pending actions stayed at 17–18 on every node, flappers included.

### 2.4 Popular-service lookups (P4)

| Window | `common-a` records per lookup (mean) | Cumulative distinct providers | `common-b` records per lookup (mean) | Cumulative distinct providers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | 9.3 | 15/38 | 5.2 | 12/35 |
| 5–10 | 23.2 | 37/38 | 22.4 | 35/35 |
| 15+ | 30.0 (cap) | 40–43/44 | 30.0 (cap) | 37–41/41 |

- **P4 refuted.**
  - A lookup walks several buckets and several registrars per bucket until it
    holds 30 ads, so the fixed first-10 per registrar does not limit coverage.
  - Every provider except flap-slow was returned at some point.
  - With 41–44 providers and a 30-record cap, a given honest provider appears in
    about 75–82 % of lookups, which is the steady-state hit rate measured.

### 2.5 Registrar, lookups, resources

- Registrar decisions:
  - 1,824 `Confirmed`, 330 `Rejected` (15 %), 10,880 `Wait`.
  - Rejections came in the 5–10 min burst (197) and smaller waves afterwards.
  - With the C1b numbers (76 % rejected at the same CPU), CPU load is **not**
    the sole driver of rejections. C5 logs the reasons.
- Lookups (8 sequential lookers):
  - 2,770 lookups, p50 3.2–5.0 s, p95 6.2–8.4 s;
  - 109 failures, all `Failed to lookup: timeout` at the module's 10 s budget, 52 of them in the first 5 min;
  - 0 empty.
- Fleet CPU: median 780 %, max 937 %. 4,146 probes, 0 failed, median 377 ms.
- Delivery bystanders: 6/6 saw all six `/logos/delivery` nodes; 114 s median to the first non-empty lookup.
