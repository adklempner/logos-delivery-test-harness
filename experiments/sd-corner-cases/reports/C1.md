# C1: hub shadow (2 common advertisers, 60 sole advertisers): report

**Verdict.** The run found a threshold the plan did not predict.
- **A libp2p_module bootstrap admits only `maxInConnections` (50) joiners.**
  Joiners after that are refused and stay isolated for good. 34 of 87 nodes
  (39 %) ran the whole 45 minutes with empty service-discovery tables, while
  every API call they made reported success.
- **Among the 53 connected nodes:**
  - every advertised (node, service) pair was found;
  - hubs did **not** slow the sole advertisers of the services they share (P2 refuted);
  - a hub's extra services took 8–30 min to become visible (P1 confirmed);
  - lookups returned the provider 87–100 % of the time from T0+5 min.

Data:
- `out/collect/sd-c1` (main run, T0 02:50:28 UTC);
- `out/collect/sd-c1-aborted` (first attempt, see §5);
- tables from `lib/sd_report.py` (`sd_report.txt`, `sd_report_connected.txt`) and `lib/sd_prom.py` (`sd_prom.txt`).

## 3. Finding 1: bootstrap saturation isolates joiners for good

**What happened.**
- From T0+21 s, the bootstrap logged `Incoming connection limit reached` on
  every further connection: 37,647 times in 45 min, about 7 per second.
  There was a burst every 10 min, at the refresh ticks.
- Joiners that were refused got a libp2p `start` reply in 0–2 s. Joiners that
  got in had a reply in 13–25 s (the 10 s start budget ran out while the DHT
  bootstrap lookup was still running) or a quick one.
- The refused ones never learnt a second peer. They kept redialling the
  bootstrap, about 400 refused dials per minute across 34 nodes, until the end.

**Who is isolated** (`cd_service_table_peers` ≤ 1 at the end, `isolated.txt`):

| Group | Isolated |
|---|---|
| hub | m1 (1 of 2) |
| solo | 12 of 40 |
| ctrl | 4 of 20 |
| late-solo, late-ctrl | **4 of 4** (joined a formed DHT at +20 min, the bootstrap still full) |
| lookers | 6 of 10 |
| delivery bystanders | **7 of 10** |

34 isolated + 52 connected members + boot = 87. 52 joiners got in against a
budget of 50 inbound connections: about the cap.

**Why it is silent.**
- `start`, `discoStart`, `discoStartAdvertising` and `discoLookup` all return
  success on an isolated node.
- Its lookups answer in 12 ms (p95 211 ms) with zero records. A connected
  node's take 2.1 s (p95 4.4 s).
- The isolated libp2p_module hosts used about 7 s of CPU in 45 min; connected
  ones 40–256 s.
- Nothing logs above DEBUG on either side, and the stage log says `start OK`.

**Effect on delivery.**
- The 10 delivery-module bystanders saw 1–3 of the 10 `/logos/delivery` nodes
  (`report.txt`: coverage median 1).
- In every earlier fleet run, members saw the full set. Those runs used a
  native logosdeliverynode bootstrap with `--max-pure-libp2p-peers=200`, not a
  libp2p_module one.

**Threshold.** The number of joiners a single libp2p_module entry node can take
equals its `maxInConnections`:
- `maxConnections / 2` in the harness, 50 here;
- libp2p_module's own default is `maxConnections 50 / maxIn 25`.

Kad keeps its connections open and nothing trims them, so slots do not free up
as the DHT forms. A node that knows only one entry has no fallback.

**Not tested here:** more than one bootstrap per member (`joinAll`) and churn freeing
slots. For the rest of the campaign the bootstrap gets `P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`, so
that this corner case does not mask the others. C1b reruns this case that way.

| Prediction | Result |
|---|---|
| P1: services 2..k from one node wait about E per registrar | **Confirmed in shape.** The hub's first service was visible at 4:25. Its other 42 needed 5–32 min, and its hit rate climbed linearly for 40 min (registrars serialize them through the IP lower bound). Registrar side (`sd_prom.txt`, all 87 nodes):
- 1,468 `Confirmed`, 668 `Rejected` and 6,328 `Wait` responses in 45 min.
- Rejections came in bursts at 5–10 min (248) and 25–30 min (327): the first
  ticket retries and the renewal retries. Ticket retries outside the 1 s window
  (P5) are the likely cause. The rejection reason is logged at TRACE only, so
  this is not proven.
- Ads per registrar: median 13, max 33. The cache cap of 1000 is far away:
  occupancy plays no role at this size.

Resources:
- Fleet CPU: median 391 %, max 884 % of 10 CPUs.
- Busiest container: m74 at 106 % (a parallel looker).
- libp2p_module RSS: median 40 → 44 MiB over 45 min (slow growth, not a leak signal at this length).
- 3,708 responsiveness probes, 0 failed (median 168 ms).
- Hub m2 held 630 pending advertiser actions (43 services × 3 registrars per bucket).

## 5. Finding 3: logoscore daemon crashes on lookers (first attempt)

The first C1 attempt (`out/collect/sd-c1-aborted`, aborted at T0+12 min)
lost 7 of 10 lookers.
- The logoscore daemon died with `Segmentation fault`, exit 139, or SIGBUS,
  exit 135, 1–8 min into the run.
- Each crash came after `RPC_FAILED` / `METHOD_FAILED` replies to concurrent
  `discoLookup` calls (5 at once per looker).
- Advertisers making the same API calls once never crashed.
- The log rate was not a flood: 60–200 lines/s, about 2,000 lines in total
  before the crash.

Isolation test `out/collect/sd-iso`: 47 nodes, 3 parallel and 3 sequential
lookers, 11 min, **no crash**.

Second C1 run (4 parallel, 6 sequential lookers): **no crash** in 45 min.

The crash is real but not reproduced. Concurrency alone does not trigger it at
47 nodes. The open suspects are concurrent CLI calls under host load, and an
interaction with the saturated bootstrap: the aborted run had the same cap.
It is logged as an open stability issue of the logoscore daemon, the same
signature as the crash after the KadDHT liveness spin.

## 6. Anomalies

- `Starting kad-disco twice` (WRN, once, boot): libp2p_module's `start` already starts
  service discovery, so the agent's own `discoStart` is redundant. Harmless.
- 30 nodes logged `libp2p start reply: Failed to start libp2p: timeout`. The 10 s start
  budget ran out during the DHT bootstrap; all of them came up and were connected.
  A timed-out start was a sign of health in this run, a fast one a sign of isolation.

## 7. C1b: rerun with an unsaturated bootstrap (`P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`)

Same manifest, except that the bootstrap gets 400 connections (200 inbound).
- T0 03:38:33 UTC, 2700 s, data in `out/collect/sd-c1b`.
- **No node isolated:** all 82 nodes reporting had non-empty service tables. 0 `connection limit` lines.

| Providers | Found | Min | Median | p90 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| solo (hub-shared service) | 40/40 | 0:41 | **6:32** | 8:52 | 17:23 |
| ctrl (no hub) | 20/20 | 0:01 | **4:40** | 9:54 | 10:36 |
| late-solo (joined the formed DHT at +20 min) | 2/2 | 2:01 | 2:01 | 5:53 | 5:53 |
| late-ctrl (+20 min) | 2/2 | 5:58 | 5:58 | 6:04 | 6:04 |
| hub, its 43 services (86 pairs) | **30/86** | 5:32 | **26:49** | 38:49 | 40:45 |

Hit rate: ctrl and solo reached 95–100 % from 15 min. **The hubs were at 0–25 % for the whole 45 min.**

- **P2 refuted again.** Shadowed and control sole advertisers become findable on the same scale:
  - p90 8:52 vs 9:54;
  - the median gap of 1:50 is within the spread;
  - all 60 were found.
- **P1 confirmed, and it worsens with fleet size.**
  - With 87 connected nodes, a node advertising 43 services had only 30 of its 86
    (hub, service) pairs visible after 45 min.
  - The hub's first-found service is not `sd/common`: that one surfaced at 36:44.
  - In C1, with 53 connected nodes, the hubs reached 100 % by 40 min.
  - Every registrar a hub registers at holds the hub's IP after the first
    accepted ad, so every further service from that IP waits about E there.
    More registrars per service means more such serial waits.
- **P5: rejections exceed confirmations under load.**

  | Run | Fleet CPU (median, of 1000 %) | Confirmed | Rejected | Rejected / (Rejected + Confirmed) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | C1 (53 connected) | 391 % | 1,468 | 668 | 31 % |
  | C1b (87 connected) | **796 %** | 1,616 | **5,190** | **76 %** |

  - Rejections come in waves 10 min apart, at 5–10, 15–20, 25–30 and 35–40 min:
    exactly when the first tickets come due, and then the renewals.
  - A rejected advertiser drops its task and retries only at the next 10-min
    maintenance round, which is why the waves repeat.
  - The rejection reason is logged at TRACE only. Ticket retries landing outside
    the 1 s window under CPU saturation remain the explanation that fits the
    timing; C3's CPU burn tests it directly.
- **Lookups hit the module's 10 s call budget.**
  - 5–20 % of lookups per window failed with `Failed to lookup: timeout`, every one
    of them at 10.0–11.5 s.
  - Successful lookups: p50 1.1–2.8 s, p95 6.2–8.3 s.
  - A lookup for a rare service walks every non-empty bucket. At 87 nodes under
    this load, it runs into libp2p_module's synchronous call limit.
- **The daemon crash reproduced.** All 4 parallel lookers (m73–m76) segfaulted (exit 139)
  between T0+94 s and T0+143 s.
  - Each crash followed concurrent `discoLookup` calls that ended at 20.2–21.0 s with
    `RPC_FAILED` / `METHOD_FAILED`, the logoscore RPC deadline.
  - The 6 sequential lookers never had a call over 11.5 s and never crashed.
  - Leading hypothesis: a module reply that arrives after the daemon's RPC timeout
    crashes the daemon. Not proven: no core dump.
  - C2–C4 run sequential lookers only.
- **Delivery bystanders healthy:** 10/10 saw all 10 `/logos/delivery` nodes. Median 62 s
  from backend ready to first non-empty lookup.
- **Resources:** probes 3,538, 4 failed, median 370 ms; fleet CPU median 796 %, max 965 %.
  The host was close to saturation for the whole run.
