# C3: scale ramp (30 → 99 agents) and the 1 s ticket window: report

**Verdict.** Discovery degrades steadily with fleet size on this host.
- Lookup p50 grows 6× from 30 to 90 nodes (0.47 → 2.8 s).
- 6–12 % of lookups hit the module's 10 s call budget once past 70 nodes.
- Registrar rejections go from **0** at 30 nodes to more than half of all
  decisions at 90.
- A **shared** service gets slower to register as more nodes advertise it:
  median time-to-findable 4:30 → 15:06. Each node's **unique** service stays
  flat at about 7 min.
- CPU saturation, from the burn and from size, raises the rejection share
  further. This fits P5, the 1 s ticket window.
- Two more lookers died with the logoscore daemon segfault, with one lookup at
  a time, after a run of module-side 10 s lookup timeouts.

Data in `out/collect/sd-c3`, T0 05:10:23 UTC, 3300 s. Pins as in C1. The
bootstrap has `P2P_MAX_CONNS=400`; lookers make one call at a time.

- Fleet CPU jumps between 39 and 59 nodes and then sits near the 10-CPU ceiling.
- Lookers slow down: 118 → 63 lookups/min. Each lookup takes longer, and one
  looker makes one call at a time.
- The probe is `getNodeInfo`, answered on libp2p_module's own thread. Its
  latency shows event-loop congestion growing with size: 38 ms → 784 ms median,
  up to 15 s.
- Every lookup failure is `Failed to lookup: timeout` at 10.0–11.5 s, the
  module's synchronous call budget. Above about 70 nodes this budget is
  regularly too short for a rare-service lookup on this host.

## 3. Registration

### 3.1 Time-to-findable, per wave and service type (from first successful advertise to first lookup returning the node)

| Wave (fleet size at join) | `sd/all`, shared: median / p90 / max | `sd/u-<host>`, unique: median / p90 / max |
|---|---|---|
| wave-0 (30) | **4:30** / 15:59 / 19:16 | 7:24 / 8:36 / 24:35 |
| wave-10 (50) | **6:42** / 13:51 / 22:31 | 7:35 / 7:53 / 15:46 |
| wave-20 (70) | **13:20** / 25:05 / 26:14 | 7:07 / 25:33 / 26:49 |
| wave-30 (91) | **15:06** / 17:17 / 20:08 (1 never) | 7:22 / 17:10 / 17:33 |

- **The shared service slows with its own popularity.** Every registrar holding
  `sd/all` ads has `boundService[sd/all]` raised by whichever advertiser
  last got a ticket (`registrar.nim:117-125`). A newcomer waits out that floor,
  and the floor is pushed forward by every new registrant. By wave-30, about
  70 nodes already advertise `sd/all`, and joiners need about E (15 min).
- **Unique services stay flat** at about 7 min, independent of size. Nothing else
  advertises them, so no service floor applies; only the IP score and the P1
  self-penalty do.
- P1 applies to every node here (two services, one IP). The p90/max values near
  15–25 min in both columns are the second service waiting about E at
  registrars where the first was accepted.
- `sd/all` coverage reached 89/90 providers by 50 min. The one never returned
  is m71, wave-30, whose `sd/all` registration never completed in the 25 min it
  had.

- **Rejections appear only once the fleet passes about 60 nodes and the host is
  saturated.** They come in the retry windows (25–30, 35–40, 45–50) and peak
  during the burn.
- **P5 is supported:**
  - At 30–59 nodes, with the host not yet saturated (49 % CPU at 39 nodes),
    there were no rejections.
  - At 99 nodes and 830 % CPU, more than half of all decisions were rejections.
  - Under the burn, the ratio went from 57 % to 76 %.
- **The cause is not proven here:** the reason is logged at TRACE only. C5 reruns
  the C1b workload on a registrar that logs it.

## 4. Logoscore daemon segfault without concurrency

m92 and m97, two lookers making one call at a time, died 2 s apart at
T0+1885/1887 s with `Segmentation fault logoscore daemon` (exit 139). The
burn had not started.
- Their last four lookups each failed with `Failed to lookup: timeout` after
  11.0–13.3 s, back to back.
- The other lookers survived similar timeouts, so a timeout is not sufficient on its own.
- Combined with C1/C1b, every daemon crash so far followed discovery calls that
  ended in a timeout: the module's 10 s budget or logoscore's 20 s RPC deadline.

Hypothesis, to be shown with a core dump: a late reply to a call that has
already timed out reaches freed state in the daemon's RPC path.

## 5. Other observations

- m55's libp2p did not come up within 180 s when wave-20 joined, at about 730 %
  fleet CPU. Its start failed for good, and nothing retries it.
- The fleet held 99 nodes for 25 min without isolation once the bootstrap had room.
